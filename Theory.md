# FutureView Profitability Learning Theory

## 1. Objective

FutureView studies a **fixed trading strategy**. The Strategy is not being optimized in the present framework.

The long-term objective is:

> Given only historical price-volume information available before a legal Strategy decision point, estimate the probability and magnitude of future Strategy profitability.

Before attempting that predictive problem, the current research stage asks a more basic question:

> Do the historical outcome descriptors generated by the fixed Strategy admit a stable low-dimensional representation?

No CNN or price-volume representation is assumed at this stage.

## 2. Historical Strategy outcomes

For a fixed historical evaluation window \(W\), let

\[
\mathcal E_W=\{E_1,E_2,\ldots,E_n\}
\]

be the realized profits of the legal Strategy outcomes observed in that window.

These outcomes are consequences of applying the fixed Strategy to the historical price-volume environment. They are not manually selected examples.

## 3. Profitability bounds

Define

\[
L_W=\min_i E_i,
\]

\[
U_W=\max_i E_i,
\]

and

\[
C_W=U_W-L_W.
\]

For \(C_W>0\), define the normalized distance of outcome \(i\) from the observed upper-profit bound as

\[
Q_i=\frac{U_W-E_i}{C_W}.
\]

Therefore

\[
E_i=U_W \Rightarrow Q_i=0,
\]

and

\[
E_i=L_W \Rightarrow Q_i=1.
\]

The collection \(\{Q_i\}\) is a distribution over legal Strategy outcomes within the historical window, not a single scalar property unless explicitly summarized.

## 4. Baseline family

A historical window may also be described by a family of reference outcomes

\[
\mathcal B_W=\{B_W^{(1)},B_W^{(2)},\ldots,B_W^{(k)}\}.
\]

Examples include:

- market / buy-and-hold baseline;
- fixed periodic-investment baseline;
- random-entry baseline;
- Strategy-null baseline in which Entry selection is neutralized while downstream rules are retained;
- matched-random baseline under comparable market conditions.

Each baseline must have a clear null/reference interpretation and be reproducible.

For baseline \(j\), define upper-profit opportunity above that baseline as

\[
A_W^{(j)}=U_W-B_W^{(j)}.
\]

This is distinct from

\[
C_W=U_W-L_W,
\]

which measures the spread between the best and worst observed Strategy outcomes.

## 5. Why a low-dimensional representation may exist

The quantities

\[
L_W,U_W,C_W,\mathcal B_W,\text{summaries of }Q_W,\ldots
\]

are different descriptions of the same historical Strategy outcome environment.

Some contain exact algebraic redundancy. For example,

\[
C_W=U_W-L_W.
\]

Others may contain empirical redundancy. For example, several different baselines may move together because they respond to the same underlying market environment.

Therefore a large descriptor vector need not imply a high-dimensional Strategy state.

Let

\[
Y_W
\]

denote the historical descriptor vector for window \(W\). The current hypothesis is only that there may exist a lower-dimensional representation

\[
Z_W=f(Y_W),
\]

with

\[
\dim Z \ll \dim Y,
\]

while preserving most of the information needed to reconstruct \(Y_W\).

No semantic meaning is assigned to the coordinates of \(Z\) in advance.

## 6. Current Autoencoder hypothesis

The Autoencoder is used only to test whether the descriptor space is low-dimensional:

\[
Y_W \xrightarrow{\text{Encoder}} Z_W \xrightarrow{\text{Decoder}} \hat Y_W,
\]

with the reconstruction requirement

\[
\hat Y_W\approx Y_W.
\]

The Autoencoder is **not** initially trained to maximize profit prediction, win-rate separation, information strength, tradable value, or any predefined interpretation of \(Z\).

The first question is simply:

> Does a small \(Z\) exist on chronological held-out historical data?

## 7. Known-redundancy control

A low reconstruction error can be misleading if the descriptor vector contains many exact algebraic copies of the same information.

Therefore low-dimensionality should be tested both with:

1. a full descriptor set; and
2. a core descriptor set with known exact algebraic redundancies removed.

For example, if both \(L\), \(U\), and \(C=U-L\) are supplied, the experiment should also be repeated without \(C\).

A low-dimensional result is more meaningful if it persists after such trivial redundancy is removed.

## 8. Profit is observed only after Z is formed

The current research order is deliberately:

\[
Y_W
\rightarrow
Z_W
\rightarrow
\text{post-hoc observation of profitability statistics}.
\]

Statistics such as mean realized profit, median realized profit, and win rate may be withheld from the Autoencoder input and examined only after \(Z\) has been learned.

No relationship between \(Z\) and these withheld profitability statistics is assumed beforehand.

Only after a low-dimensional \(Z\) is established do we ask whether different regions or coordinates of \(Z\) show stable relationships with final realized profitability.

## 9. Sequential Strategy decision states

If a Strategy contains Entry followed by Add decisions, the later decision states are nested conditional subsets.

For example,

\[
S_{\mathrm{Add}}\subseteq S_{\mathrm{Entry}}.
\]

A path that reaches Add is still part of the Entry population, but after Add occurs the relevant probability space is conditional on that event.

The same general framework may later be applied separately to Entry and conditional Add states, with bounds and \(Q\) defined relative to the appropriate population.

This is not part of the first low-dimensionality test.

## 10. Price-volume data and CNN are later stages

All conventional technical indicators are transformations of underlying price and volume. The eventual predictive model is therefore intended to learn directly from historical price-volume data rather than depend on a large manually selected indicator set.

However, the CNN problem is intentionally postponed.

The next stage is justified only if the historical descriptor space first demonstrates a meaningful and stable low-dimensional structure.

## 11. Current research sequence

The research sequence is now fixed as:

1. generate historical legal Strategy outcomes;
2. compute \(L,U,C,Q\)-distribution descriptors and a small family of meaningful baselines;
3. test whether those descriptors admit a stable low-dimensional \(Z\) using an unsupervised Autoencoder;
4. only after \(Z\) is formed, observe withheld profit statistics and test for relationships;
5. only if that structure is meaningful, return to the question of how raw price-volume information may map into it.

No CNN architecture, latent semantic interpretation, gate, or profitability prediction head is assumed before Step 4 is established empirically.
